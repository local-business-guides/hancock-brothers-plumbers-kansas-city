What to prepare and ask

A plumber may need to look at the affected area to determine what’s going on. The right approach depends on the problem, so avoid assuming that a symptom points to one specific repair. Be ready to explain when you first noticed it and whether anything has changed since then.

Before calling, make a short checklist:

  • Write down the fixture or room involved and the main symptom.
  • Note when you noticed the issue and any changes you’ve observed.
  • Mention any steps you’ve already tried, without taking apart equipment.
  • Ask what information or access would help with an inspection.
  • Confirm the recommended work and any expected charges before authorizing it.

Avoid describing a guess as a confirmed diagnosis. Also, don’t delay a call just because you can’t identify the source. If water is actively escaping, a fixture has stopped working, or the problem is getting worse, contact a plumber and explain what’s happening. If you can safely limit further water flow, do so, but don’t attempt repairs that could create another problem.
